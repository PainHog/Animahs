# Art checklist: every piece must pass every item

This list comes from mistakes found in Richard Moore's first three games: *Heisty Spideys*, *Don't Get Forked* and *Nine Lives Gang*. Every item below was a real defect in a real book.
- **Floating or partly-supported objects:**
  - a pencil half on a notepad;
  - a decal floating off a tin;
  - a towel with no rack;
  - a parrot cage hanging from nothing;
  - plant leaves floating over a pot;
  - a skylight half off a roof;
  - a wheelie bin on one wheel.
- **Anatomy:**
  - a cat with one eye;
  - a cat with three legs;
  - a dog with no eye;
  - a human with no hands.
- **Consistency:**
  - characters whose markings flip sides between pictures;
  - a hook cut off by the frame.
- **Physics and perspective:**
  - a bottle cap at an impossible angle;
  - a launcher that couldn't work;
  - plates overhanging a table.
- **Scale:** cats twice their real size next to doors.
- **Stand-in pictures:** public-domain prints that read as offensive caricatures.

## Physics
1. **Gravity and support.** Every object rests on a surface, hangs from a mounted fixture, or is visibly held. Nothing floats.
   - **Partial support counts as floating.** Check every end and edge of every object. A pencil, spoon, stick, card or board resting on a surface at one end must be supported at the other end too, or lie wholly on the surface, or be propped or held.
   - **Overhangs:** an object overhanging an edge must have its centre of mass over the support.
   - **Objects resting on other objects** (a plate on a table, a cup on a saucer) must lie within the lower object's outline.
   - **Hanging things** (cages, lamps, signs) meet their hook or arm with no gap. A ring sits on the hook.
   - **Labels and decals** must read as printed on their object, never as a separate floating thing.
   - **Plants** have stems that reach into soil.
2. **Contact.** Feet, bases, wheels and edges touch their surface, with a contact shadow. A wheeled object (a bin, a cart) stands on all its wheels, or on its wheels plus a foot.
3. **Ropes, chains, threads and cables.** Anchored at both ends to something real (or leaving the frame). A strand attached to an object (yarn to a ball, a lead to a collar) actually joins it. It hangs straight down under a weight and sags between two anchors.
4. **Angles and perspective.** Flat things lie flat on the ground plane, drawn as ellipses that match the scene's eye level. Roof features sit on the roof plane. Everything shares one horizon.
5. **Mechanisms work.** Any device must be buildable and usable as drawn.
6. **Fluids and particles.** Drips fall, steam and smoke rise, crumbs lie on surfaces.

## Completeness
7. **No half-finished objects.** Every object is drawn completely, or is obviously and deliberately cropped by the frame edge. Nothing important is clipped.
8. **Detail.** Scenes have the supporting detail the place needs, without clutter that competes with the subject. Don't reuse the same lone spot picture to fill empty pages.

## Anatomy and characters
9. **Counts.** Count, don't glance: eyes (both visible when the face is frontal), ears, arms, legs, fingers and tails. Hands have 5 fingers, with the thumb on the correct side. Limbs join the body where they should. Animals stand in poses a real animal can hold.
10. **One design per recurring character.** Same silhouette, proportions, colours, clothing and markings everywhere.
    - **Keep a reference sheet per recurring character:** front view, left profile and right profile, with every asymmetric feature labelled (which ear is notched, which eye has the patch, which paw has the sock).
    - **Never mirror-flip a character with asymmetric markings** to make a facing-the-other-way pose; redraw the markings on the correct side.
    - Check every new picture against the sheet.
11. **Size matches the text.** If the text says "enormous", "large and fluffy" or "a kitten", the drawing shows it.
12. **Scale.** Characters, doors, furniture and props are in true proportion within a scene and across the book. Measure a doorway or chair against the character.
13. **Original characters only.** Never echo famous cartoon or film characters (Garfield, Top Cat, the Universal monster make-ups and so on), or brand mascots. A logo must not sit close to an existing brand.

## Stand-in and public-domain pictures
14. **Sensitivity pass.** Look at every person in old prints at print size for caricature: hooked "comic" noses, blacked-out faces, racial or religious stereotypes. Replace any that could read that way, whatever the artist meant.
15. **Fit and quality.** Each picture shows what it illustrates. It's at least 250 ppi at print size, with no plate marks or stray body parts at the crop edge. Pairs meant to be one character in two states look like the same character.
16. **Source log.** For every picture, log the artist, title, date, institution, link or object ID, and the licence as downloaded. Prefer CC0 museum releases. Confirm the publication date of any artist who died after 1930.

## Colour and legibility
17. **Palette** colours only. Dark subjects on dark grounds need separation: a rim light, a lighter backdrop or a light outline.
18. **Never code meaning by colour alone.** "Watched", "danger" and the like also get a symbol, so they survive black-and-white printing.

## Maps and diagrams
19. **Labels** are at least 7.5 pt, in the book's fonts (never a fallback font). Labels don't collide with each other or with door-swing arcs. Arrows and ways out stay inside the frame. The map matches its room table, room by room. Closed doors are drawn as the legend says.

## Text
20. **No garbled pseudo-text** in pictures; squiggles may stand in for writing.

## How to audit (mandatory method)
For each picture, write an **object table**: every object (small props, crumbs, tools, furniture and each character's feet), its contact points, and what is under or holding each one. Any row with an unsupported end, edge or foot, or an object outside its support's outline, is a defect.

Add a **character row** for each figure:
- count its eyes, ears, limbs, fingers and tail;
- for a recurring character, check it against the reference sheet, with left and right sides compared explicitly.

Look at every render yourself at print size. Viewing the picture as a whole isn't enough: almost every mistake found was a small prop at the edge of a scene, or a marking on the wrong side.

## Project-specific: Morph Game (codename)
21. **Real animals are drawn true to life.** Use the right species markings: a jaguar's rosettes have spots inside them and a leopard's don't; a cheetah has tear lines. Get the anatomy, proportions, number of toes and claws, and the stance right. Use a photo reference for every species, and note which reference was used.
22. **Size is true.** A morphed character is the real size of the animal, and that size is consistent next to people, furniture and other animals.
23. **Each character is recognisable in every form.** A character keeps one visual tie between their human form and their animal form, such as an eye colour, a scar or a collar, everywhere they appear.
24. **Transformation art suits the tone and age range** agreed in the design. No accidental body horror.
25. **Original designs and trade dress only.** Don't echo *Animorphs*: no human-to-animal morph-sequence covers (the series' signature look), no blue centaur aliens, no slug parasites in ears, no lettering styled like the series logo. Don't copy the look of other shapeshifter games either.
