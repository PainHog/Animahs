Chapter 12

Making Missions

Once you’ve played the missions in Part Three, you’ll want your own: the Hunt at the swimming baths, the Hunt at the school fair, the Hunt in your actual town. This chapter shows how to build one that plays well. The shape is always the same: two ways in, a few obstacles, an objective, and a way out.

The Checklist

Every Mission Has

  • A brief: what the Hunt is doing and what the team must do: free, wreck or steal.
  • A map that matches the obstacles, place by place.
  • Two ways in with different tags and approaches, so they suit different forms and scouting matters.
  • Two to four obstacles inside, then the objective.
  • One or two Escape obstacles, each with at least two approaches, so no team is stuck without a way out.
  • For every obstacle: its tags, every approach that works and its Difficulty, point or group, the progress needed, and any guards or danger.
  • Stats for every threat (use Chapter 10).
  • A Limit for 3, 4 and 5 players, and a label (Easy, Standard or Hard).
  • At least one moment for human form: someone to talk past, a lock to work, a crowd to blend into.

Choosing Obstacles

Give most obstacles two or three approaches, so different animals can solve them in different ways. A wall might be Move 2 (climb or fly it), Sneak 3 (find the gap) or Force 3 (break through). Then give it tags, and think about who they favour:

Across a mission, try to use every tag at least once, and don’t let one tag take over. A mission that is all rooftops is a mission for birds.

Difficulty

  • 1: routine, for a quick roll that rarely goes wrong.
  • 2: the normal Difficulty for anything worth rolling.
  • 3: hard, the approach that works but isn’t the obvious one.
  • 4: almost never on its own; Weaknesses and Trackers will push some rolls there anyway.

Point or group?

If one person could deal with it for everyone (open a door, find the path), make it a point obstacle needing 1 to 3 progress. If everyone has to get past it themselves (cross a yard, climb a wall), make it a group obstacle. Group obstacles are harder with more players, point obstacles easier; that’s what the per-size Limit is for.

Guards and danger

Guard one or two obstacles per mission: a Beater at the door, two Hounds on the lawn, a Huntsman at the objective. A dangerous obstacle (a fast river, a high beam) deals 1 Hurt on a Fail. Use it where a fall or a current makes sense.

Setting the Limit

The Limit is what makes a mission Easy or Hard. The five missions in Part Three were tested thousands of times, with groups of 3, 4 and 5, so their Limits hit their labels. For a new mission:

  1. Find the most similar mission in Part Three: same number of obstacles, similar Difficulties, similar guards. Start from its Limits.
  2. For each extra obstacle you add, add 1 to each Limit. For each one fewer, take 1 away.
  3. Play it, and adjust. If the Hunt is Up in nearly every game, add 1; if it’s never Up, take 1 away.

Designer’s Note

Steps 2 and 3 are a rule of thumb, not something we’ve tested. A mission’s label should come from how it actually plays, not from what you meant it to be. If your Easy mission keeps going wrong, it’s a Standard mission, and that’s fine.
