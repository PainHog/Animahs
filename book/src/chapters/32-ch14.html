Chapter 14 · Standard mission

The Zoo After Dark

The Hunt’s Trappers are crating the zoo’s animals at night and driving them to the Lodge. Tonight it’s the snow leopards. The team gets into the zoo, finds the Trappers, slips past the Hound patrol and breaks open the crates before the truck leaves.

Read This Aloud

The zoo has been closed for an hour. From the river path you can hear engines where there shouldn’t be any: a truck with its lights off, backing up to the loading dock. A woman in a waxed jacket is checking a clipboard. On the side of a crate, in fresh chalk: SNOW LEOPARD ×2 — THORNHILL. You have until they finish loading.

What’s Going On

Dr Hale, the zoo’s director, is a Huntsman. She’s been quietly sending the zoo’s animals up to the Lodge, where the Hunt uses them to train its Hounds and to tempt the Bonded. Two Trappers are loading tonight’s crates. A pair of Hounds patrol the grounds, and an ordinary night keeper (who knows nothing about the Hunt) is on the staff gate.

The Places

Way in A: The Perimeter Fence

A tall wire fence along the open car park. It’s easy to climb or fly, but anyone sneaking along it is in plain view of the road.

Way in B: The River Culvert

A drain runs under the fence from the river, dark and half full of cold water. The current is strong: a Fail here deals 1 Hurt.

Inside 1: Find the Trappers

The zoo is big and dark. From the aviary walkway, high above the paths, you can watch for torches and listen for the truck. It takes a sharp pair of eyes, or a good nose.

Inside 2: The Night Keeper

Old Mr Dunmore on the staff gate is no Huntsman, just a tired man with a flask of tea. A kid with a good story (“My mum’s a volunteer and I left my bag in the café”) can talk their way past. Sneaking past is harder; he has nothing else to look at. An animal can’t talk, so it’s Sneak or nothing for them.

Inside 3: The Hound Patrol

Two Hounds walk the lawns between the staff gate and the loading dock. Everyone has to cross. Anyone who watches the patrol long enough can find the gap in it (Sense 3). If anyone Fails, the Hounds turn, and it’s a Clash.

The Objective: The Loading Dock

Six crates on the dock, two of them already in the truck, and two Trappers working by lamplight. The crates are locked with padlocks and bolts: open them with clever hands or break them. If anyone Fails while the Trappers are there, it’s a Clash, and in it their nets come out. Beating them doesn’t open the crates: you still need the rest of the 3 progress. When the last crate opens, the snow leopards bound away into the dark.

The Escape: Across the Moat

The fastest way out is across the old moat around the big cat enclosure and up onto the river path. It’s cold, wet and wide open.

Threats

Two Hounds on the patrol and two Trappers at the dock (Chapter 10). The night keeper isn’t a threat; if he sees an animal he just phones the zoo vet. If the Hunt is Up, Dr Hale arrives with a Master, and the truck’s headlights snap on.

Spotlights

Birds shine finding the Trappers from the High, Open aviary walkway. A Reptile is at home in the culvert and the moat. The staff gate is easiest for a kid in human form who can talk. Big Cats hate the moat’s Water: they can cross it as kids instead.

After the Mission

  • Win: the snow leopards are back in their enclosure by morning, and the truck leaves empty. Dr Hale starts checking who’s been visiting the zoo.
  • Costly Win: the leopards are free, but a friend went up the hill in a crate instead.
  • Loss: the truck leaves for Thornhill. The Hunt now has two more animals to train its Hounds with.
