Chapter 9

Running the Game

As the Storyteller, you are the town, the Hunt, the night, and every voice that isn’t a player’s. You describe what the Bonded see, smell and hear; you say when a roll is needed and how hard it is; and you play the Hunt fairly but without mercy. You never roll dice. Your job is to set problems; the players’ job is to solve them.

The First Night

Before Everyone Arrives (about 15 minutes)

  • Print the six ready-made Bonded (Chapter 18) or one blank character sheet per player, one mission tracker, the Storyteller’s round card, and the At the Table page (Part Four).
  • Gather six d6 per player and about six Wild tokens each, plus a marker for the Scent track.
  • Read Pemberton’s Pets (Chapter 13), the Easy mission. Note its Limit for your number of players.

At the Table (about 15 minutes)

  1. Talk about the feel (two minutes): see “Keeping It Fun” below.
  2. Hand out characters. Each player takes a ready-made Bonded. If two players want the same one, they roll a die: the higher roll chooses first (roll again on a tie). A player who’d rather make their own does it now, with Chapter 2’s steps; allow ten minutes.
  3. Explain the roll with one practice roll each: three dice against Difficulty 2. Count the 5s and 6s, and compare them to the Difficulty.
  4. Give everyone 4 Wild (5 for a Gamer) and explain the four ways to spend it.
  5. Read the brief and start.

Players don’t read the mission pages. Show them the map and the two ways in; tell them an obstacle’s approaches and Difficulties when they reach it.

Running an Obstacle

  1. Describe it, with its tags: “The shop floor is pitch dark. The watchman is asleep by the counter.”
  2. Say what works: “Sneak or Move, Difficulty 2. It’s a group obstacle: everyone has to get past.”
  3. Ask who does what. Let the players decide forms, who rolls and who Helps.
  4. Resolve the rolls: result first, then its cost.
  5. Mark Scent as the rules say, and describe the change: a dog barks two streets away; a light comes on upstairs.

If a player wants to try something that isn’t on the obstacle’s list, pick the nearest approach and Difficulty from the list. Don’t make up a new approach that skips the obstacle: every way through is a roll.

Running a Clash

Use the round card in Part Four. It has four steps:

A Clash Round

  1. Everyone in the Clash takes one action, in any order the players like. Help can come at any time.
  2. Resolve each roll as it’s made: result, then Harm.
  3. Each threat nobody rolled against this round hurts one character of your choice.
  4. Check the end: every threat out, nobody left, or the end of round 4. Then raise Scent once: +1, or +2 if it reached round 4, someone went Down, or someone shifted during it.

When a threat attacks someone, choose fairly. Pick whoever the threat would really go for, such as the one who just hit it or the one closest to it, rather than always the weakest.

The Scent Track

Keep the Scent track where everyone can see it. The Hunt closing in should be a shared, growing dread, not a secret. When Scent reaches half the Limit, tell the players: “They’re On the Trail. The way out will be harder.” When it reaches the Limit, make it big: horns, lights, the baying of Hounds. The Hunt is Up.

Making Rulings

The rules don’t cover everything. When something comes up:

  • Choose the reading nearest to what the rules already say, and say it out loud so everyone knows.
  • Never make a roll free. No plan, perk or trick lets anyone skip an obstacle without a roll.
  • Write it down and keep that ruling for the rest of the campaign. We’d love to hear about it too.

Keeping It Fun

This game is for ages 10 and up. Some players will want the Hunt to be properly scary; others won’t. Before the first mission, ask:

  • Is there anything nobody wants in the game? Snakes, spiders, cages, the dark. Take it out or keep it off-screen.
  • How scary should the Hunt be? Creepy-and-cold, or bluster-and-moustache?

During play, anyone can say “pause” at any time, for any reason, and the game stops while you talk. Then carry on, change direction, or skip ahead. Nobody has to explain why.

Keep the violence light. Hits are scrapes and bruises; a threat that’s “out” is knocked over, chased off or tangled in its own net. Nobody bleeds and nobody dies.

Giving Every Animal Its Moment

Look at the team’s frames before a mission, and notice the tags. A mission with no Water gives an alligator little to do; one with no Narrow gaps leaves a mouse on the bench. The missions in this book spread their tags so that every frame has a place to shine. When you build your own (Chapter 12), do the same.

Pacing

  • One roll at a time at a point obstacle. If someone fancies a turn, let them have the next round.
  • Move quickly between obstacles. A sentence of description, then the next problem.
  • Remind players to spend Wild. Every token left at the end is wasted.
  • A mission takes two to three hours. If you’re running long, it’s fine to describe the last stretch of the Escape after its final roll.
