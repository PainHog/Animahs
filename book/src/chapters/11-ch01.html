Chapter 1

The Wild Wakes

The storm took the town’s lights out at ten past nine. By half past, six kids who had never met were each staring into the eyes of an animal, and nothing was ever the same again.

You’re an ordinary kid in an ordinary town, and you can turn into an animal. Not any animal: your animal, the one that looked back at you on the night the Wild woke up. A jaguar on the wet roof of the garden centre. An owl on your windowsill. A coyote at the end of the street, waiting, as if it knew your name.

You didn’t ask for it. Nobody chose you, nobody handed you a strange gadget, and nobody explained anything. The Wild simply woke in you, the way spring wakes a seed. And a few days later you found out why: somebody in town is hunting.

What This Game Is

Second Nature is a roleplaying game. One person is the Storyteller, who describes the world and plays everyone else in it. Everyone else plays one Bonded kid: a young person, aged 11 to 15, who can become one real animal. Together you tell the story of what happens when your group takes on the Gilded Hunt.

The Hunt is a secret society of rich, respectable people who hunt the Wild itself. For three hundred years they have trapped Bonded animals and worn their power as Trophies: a gold ring that gives a man a hawk’s eyes, a brooch that lends a woman a wolf’s nose. Now they are planning the Last Hunt, to chain the Old Wild for good. Every animal on Earth would come to heel. And after the animals, the people.

The Wild woke you to stop them.

How a Game Night Goes

Each session is one mission: a night when the team sneaks into one of the Hunt’s places to free what’s caged there, wreck what they’ve built, or steal back what they’ve taken. A mission takes two or three hours. You scout the place, pick a way in, get past what stands between you and the objective, and get out again before the Hunt catches your scent.

You can play one mission on its own, or play a campaign: about six missions in a row, building to the night of the Last Hunt (Chapter 8).

What Makes Your Kid

Every Bonded is built from three parts:

  • A frame: the kind of animal you become, such as Big Cats, Birds or Critters. The frame gives your animal form its strengths and weaknesses.
  • An animal perk: the special gift of your exact animal. A jaguar disappears into the dark; a raven can work a latch with its beak.
  • A human perk: something you were good at before any of this. A football player still tackles like one when she’s a bison.

You have one animal, for life. You won’t collect others. Over a campaign your Bond grows deeper instead (Chapter 8).

What You Need

For the first night

  • Three to five players and a Storyteller.
  • Six ordinary six-sided dice for each player (no roll ever uses more than six), plus a few for the Storyteller.
  • About six small tokens per player for Wild: beads, buttons, coins or counters.
  • A character sheet for each player and one mission tracker for the table (Part Four), or the six ready-made Bonded in Chapter 18.
  • Pencils, and two or three hours.

Setting up the first time takes under half an hour if you use the ready-made characters. Chapter 9 has a step-by-step first night for the Storyteller.

The Feel of the Game

Second Nature is for ages 10 and up. It’s an adventure with real danger, like a good family adventure film. The Hunt is frightening, people get hurt, and things go wrong. But nobody in your group dies. The worst that can happen is being Taken: caught and caged by the Hunt. A Taken friend can be rescued, and in a campaign the rescue is often the best mission of all.

Changing shape is a wonder, and often a comedy. You can smell what the neighbours had for dinner. You can see in the dark. Your tail knocks a plant pot off the shelf. The book never treats the change as horror, and neither should your table.

Talk About It First

Before the first mission, spend two minutes agreeing what your table is happy to include. Is anyone scared of snakes or spiders? Should the Hunt be creepy, or more like cartoon villains? Chapter 9 has simple ways to check in and to pause a scene. Use them.

How to Read This Book

Part One (Chapters 1–8) is for everyone: making a character, rolling dice, shifting shape, missions, Clashes, and what happens when things go wrong. Part Two (Chapters 9–12) is for the Storyteller. Part Three (Chapters 13–17) holds five ready-made missions; players shouldn’t read it. Part Four has ready-made characters, the rules on one page, and the sheets to print.

Words to Know

  • Bonded: a kid who can become their animal.
  • The Wild: the living wildness in every animal; where your power comes from.
  • The Gilded Hunt: the enemy. Its members are Huntsmen and Masters; its servants are Beaters, Hounds and Trappers.
  • Approach: how you do something: Sneak, Force, Move, Sense or Wits.
  • Hit: a die showing 5 or 6. Difficulty: the number of hits you need.
  • Wild: your tokens, spent to shift, Push, Help and Patch Up.
  • Scent: how close the Hunt is to finding you. When it reaches the Limit, the Hunt is Up.
  • Clash: a fight.
  • Taken: caught and caged by the Hunt.

A Taste of Play

Example of Play

Maya, Priya and Theo are inside Pemberton’s Pets, where the Hunt is caging the town’s strays. A Beater, a hired watchman, dozes by the counter in the dark.

Storyteller: “The shop floor is dark. The watchman’s chair creaks every time he breathes. It’s Sneak, Difficulty 2, or Move, Difficulty 2. Everyone has to get past him. If anyone Fails, he wakes up.”

Maya is already a jaguar. Her frame’s Sneak is 4, Big Cats get +1 die in the Dark, and her jaguar’s Night Rosettes lowers the Difficulty by 1. She rolls five dice against Difficulty 1 and gets 2, 3, 5, 6 and 1: two hits. A Success. She pads past without a sound.

Priya is a barn owl. Birds only have Sneak 2, but her owl’s Silent Wings gives +1 die in the Dark, so she rolls three dice: 4, 5, 2. One hit against Difficulty 2 is a Partial. She’s past, but her wingbeat stirs a stack of feed bags.

Theo is still human, because the office door ahead needs hands. His Sneak is only 1, so Maya spends 1 Wild to Help: “I creep back and nudge him towards the quiet floorboards.” Theo rolls two dice and gets 5 and 5: a Success.

Everyone is past. One Partial means the obstacle raises the Hunt’s Scent by 1 as it ends (Chapter 5). The watchman sleeps on.
