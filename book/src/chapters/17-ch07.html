Chapter 7

Hurt, Taken and Worse

Things go wrong. Hounds bite, nets fly, and sometimes a friend doesn’t make it out. Nobody in Second Nature dies. The worst that happens is being Taken, and a Taken friend can always be rescued. Here is everything that can go wrong, from a scraped knee to a cage.

Hurt

Hurt

Everyone has 3 Hurt boxes, the same in every form. Mark one for each point of Hurt you take. Hurt never lowers your dice: a bruised kid rolls as well as a fresh one. All Hurt clears between missions.

You take Hurt from a threat’s Harm (Chapter 6), from Failing at a dangerous obstacle (Chapter 5), and from shifting with no Wild left (Chapter 4).

Patch Up

Spend 1 Wild and your action to heal 1 Hurt, yours or a friend’s. You can also Patch Up once on the way between obstacles without using an action (instead of shifting). Patching Up a Down friend lowers their Hurt, but they stay Down until the scene ends.

Down

Down

When your third Hurt box is marked, you’re Down. You can’t act until the scene ends.

When the scene ends, if a friend is still standing and the Hunt isn’t Up, you get back up with 2 Hurt and, at a group obstacle, you’re past it: your friends carried you. Otherwise you’re Taken.

If you go Down in animal form, you’re Stuck: you can’t shift back until the mission ends.

Netted

Netted

When you Fail against a Trapper in a Clash, you’re Netted instead of Hurt. You can’t act until a friend spends their action to free you (no roll). When the scene ends, you’re freed if a friend is still standing and the Hunt isn’t Up; otherwise you’re Taken.

Taken

Taken

You’re Taken:

  • when you Fail while the Hunt is Up;
  • when you’re Down or Netted at the end of a scene and nobody is standing to help, or the Hunt is Up.

The Hunt cages you, and you’re out for the rest of the mission. If you were carrying the prize, it’s lost. You’re also Exposed.

A friend rescued during a mission goes with the team: they don’t roll or Help, they can’t be Taken unless everyone else is, and they don’t count when you check whether everyone was Taken.

A Taken player isn’t out of the game. In a one-shot, they can describe what they see from the cage, and it’s worth remembering for later. In a campaign, the next mission can be the rescue (Chapter 8).

Exposed

You’re Exposed when the Hunt sees you shift, or Takes you. They know your face and your Mark now. In a campaign, an Exposed character’s next mission starts at Scent 1, because the Hunt is watching your house. That’s only ever 1: it doesn’t add to a poor scouting roll’s Scent 1.

Everyone at Once

If every character is Down, Netted or Taken at the same moment, nobody is left to help: everyone is Taken, and the mission is a Loss.

Giving Up

You can always Withdraw outside a Clash: abandon the objective and head straight for the Escape (Chapter 5). It’s a Loss, but on the way out the only way to be Taken is the usual one: a Fail once the Hunt is Up, or being left Down or Netted. Withdrawing is never better than winning, but it beats being caught.

Telling It Kindly

Describe being Taken the way a good adventure film would: a net drops, a gilded cage clicks shut, and a voice in the dark says, “Well, well. Another one.” Keep it tense, never cruel. Being Taken is a cliffhanger, and the rescue is the next chapter.
