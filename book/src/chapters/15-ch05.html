Chapter 5

Missions and the Hunt’s Scent

A mission is one night’s work against the Hunt: get in, do the job, get out. The Hunt can smell the Wild. Every slip you make brings them closer, and if they catch your scent before you’re out, the Masters come running.

The Shape of a Mission

  1. The brief. The Storyteller tells you what the Hunt is up to and what you need to do.
  2. Scouting. One of you scouts the place.
  3. The way in. Every mission has two ways in. Scouting decides who picks.
  4. Obstacles. Two to four of them stand between you and the objective.
  5. The objective. Free them, wreck it, or steal it.
  6. The Escape. One or two obstacles on the way out.
  7. The result. Win, Costly Win or Loss.

Players see the mission map and the two ways in. You learn an obstacle’s approaches and Difficulties when you reach it.

Time

  • A round is everyone getting one action: one roll, a shift, a Patch Up, or freeing a friend.
  • A scene is one obstacle, or one Clash. Scouting is a scene too, and when a Clash breaks out at an obstacle, the rest of the obstacle after it is a new scene. “Once per scene” abilities come back at the start of every scene.
  • A mission is the whole night.

Moving from one obstacle to the next is free, and on the way everyone may Shift or Patch Up once without using an action (it still costs the Wild).

Scouting

Scouting

Before going in, one character rolls Sense against the mission’s scouting Difficulty (2 in every mission in this book). Shifting to scout is out of sight, as it is between obstacles; it still costs Wild, but it doesn’t use up the scout’s free Shift before the way in. Friends may Help. Scouting happens outside the place, so it has no tags: Strengths, Weaknesses and any perk that needs a place tag don’t count, but a perk for every Sense roll (like Scout) does.

  • Critical: the team picks the way in and gains two Edges.
  • Success: the team picks the way in and gains an Edge.
  • Partial: the team picks the way in, but Scent starts at 1.
  • Fail: the Storyteller picks the way in, and Scent starts at 1.

Obstacles

An obstacle is anything that stands in your way: a guarded gate, a locked door, a dark hall full of Hounds. Each one lists:

  • its tags (Dark, High, Narrow, Water, Open, Crowd, Locked);
  • the approaches that work, with a Difficulty for each;
  • whether it’s a point or a group obstacle, and the progress a point obstacle needs;
  • whether it’s guarded (and by whom) or dangerous.

Point obstacles

One person can deal with it for everyone: a door to unlock, a path to find. Each round, one character rolls (any one of you, and it can be someone different each round) and the others may Help. Progress adds up until the obstacle is cleared, then everyone goes through.

Group obstacles

Everyone has to get past it: a yard to sneak across, a wall to climb. Each round, everyone who isn’t past yet rolls, and each gets past on their own Partial or better. Roll one at a time, in any order; each player decides on a Push before the next one rolls. Those already past can still Help, Patch Up and free a Netted friend. Down and Netted characters can’t roll: when everyone who can act is past, the obstacle is done, and the Down and Netted get up or are freed, or are Taken, as the scene ends (Chapter 7).

Results at an Obstacle

  • Success: 1 progress (or, at a group obstacle, you’re past).
  • Critical: as a Success, plus 1 extra progress. If the extra isn’t needed (always so at a group obstacle), the team gains an Edge instead.
  • Partial: as a Success, but Scent +1.
  • Fail: no progress (you’re not past), and Scent +1. A dangerous obstacle also deals 1 Hurt to whoever Failed.

At a group obstacle, Scent rises once, when the obstacle ends: +1 if anyone got a Partial or a Fail. And from the third round on, at the end of each round in which anyone still isn’t past, Scent rises by 1.

In the Escape, Partials cost no Scent. Only Fails count.

Guarded obstacles

Some obstacles have guards: Beaters, Hounds, Trappers or a Huntsman. A Fail at a guarded obstacle starts a Clash with the guards (Chapter 6). You can also choose to start the Clash yourselves, before anyone rolls; then it begins at once. When a Fail starts it at a group obstacle, finish the round’s rolls first, and the obstacle raises Scent for them then (+1 for any Partial or Fail); then the whole team is in the Clash, including anyone already past. The Fail that starts a Clash isn’t a roll against a threat, so it can’t get you Netted. If you beat the guards (or they pull back after round 4), the obstacle is cleared, unless it says progress is still needed. In that case you keep the progress you’ve made and carry on rolling, without the guards.

The team stays together. Everyone takes the same way in and faces the same obstacle at the same time; nobody can run ahead or take the other way.

Scent

The Hunt’s Scent is how close they are to finding you. Track it on the mission tracker, from 0 up to the mission’s Limit. Each mission gives its Limit for 3, 4 and 5 players.

Scent

  • It rises for Partials and Fails at point obstacles, once when a group obstacle ends (and at the end of each round from the third on while anyone isn’t past), once at the end of each Clash, for a shift the Hunt can see, for a poor scouting roll, and at the start of a mission after someone was Exposed (it starts at Scent 1, not 2 with a poor scouting roll). Nothing else raises it.
  • It never goes down. The Limit never changes during a mission.
  • On the Trail: if, when an Escape obstacle begins, Scent is at least half the Limit (round up), that Escape obstacle is at +1 Difficulty for every approach.
  • The Hunt is Up when Scent reaches the Limit. Any Scent past the Limit is lost.

The Hunt Is Up

When Scent reaches the Limit, horns sound across the dark, and the Masters of the Hunt arrive. Nobody fights a Master. You run.

When the Hunt Is Up

  • Finish resolving the roll that did it as if the Hunt weren’t Up yet: if it finished the objective, it’s finished; if it was a Fail at a guarded obstacle, its Clash still starts.
  • From now on, any Fail means you’re Taken, wherever you are. That goes for a Fail in a Clash too, instead of taking Harm. A Fail at a guarded obstacle doesn’t start a Clash any more: you’re just Taken.
  • Anyone still Down or Netted when the scene ends is Taken.
  • If the objective isn’t done, the team chooses: keep going, or Withdraw straight to the Escape (a Loss).
  • If you’re already in the Escape, carry on.

Advice: Press On or Run?

Press on only if you’re already working on the objective. Run the moment a friend is Taken. A Loss can be put right later. A whole team caught at the cage can’t.

The Objective and the Prize

The objective is an obstacle like any other: usually a point obstacle needing 2 or 3 progress, sometimes guarded. When it’s done, the job’s done and you head for the Escape.

Some objectives are a prize to steal, like a Trophy or a ledger. When the objective is done, one character there takes it, with no action, and carries it in hand or in jaws. Handing it to a friend at the same obstacle takes your action, with no roll; between obstacles it’s free. If you shift, it drops at your feet: picking it up takes an action at an obstacle or in a Clash, but between obstacles it’s free. If the carrier is Taken, the prize is lost, and so is the objective. So is a prize nobody is carrying when the last Escape obstacle is behind you.

Withdrawing

The team may Withdraw at any time outside a Clash: give up on the objective and go straight to the Escape. (In a Clash, Slip Away or see it through first.) Withdrawing before the way in just means going home: a Loss, with no Escape to cross. Either way the mission is a Loss, but you live to fight another night.

The Escape

The Escape is one or two group obstacles on the way out. Everyone still free must get past them. Partials cost no Scent here, but Fails do, and if the Hunt is Up, a Fail means you’re Taken. When everyone still free is past the last Escape obstacle, the mission ends.

How It Ends

ResultWhen
WinThe objective is done and nobody is Taken.
Costly WinThe objective is done, but someone (not everyone) is Taken.
LossThe objective isn’t done (you Withdrew, or the prize was lost), or everyone was Taken, even after the objective was done (what you did stays done, but the Hunt track still rises by 2).

When it’s over: unspent Wild and Edges are lost and Hurt clears. In a campaign, the result moves the Hunt track, everyone marks 1 Growth, and Exposed and Taken characters carry over (Chapter 8).

Example: the Hunt Is Up at the Cages

The team is at the cages in Pemberton’s Pets: Wits 2 or Force 3, 3 progress. The Limit for four players is 6, and Scent is already 5. They have 2 progress. Priya rolls Wits and gets a Partial: 1 progress, the cages spring open, and Scent +1 reaches the Limit. The result comes first, so the cages are open before the Hunt is Up. Horns sound outside. The objective is done, so there’s no choice to make: run! In the Escape, every Fail now means Taken, so the team spends every Wild token it has left on Help and Pushes.
