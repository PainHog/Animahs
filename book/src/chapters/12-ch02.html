Chapter 2

Making Your Bonded

Making a character takes about ten minutes. You choose (or roll for) your animal, decide what you’re good at as a person, and pick one small thing that shows in both your shapes. If you’d rather start straight away, take one of the six ready-made Bonded in Chapter 18.

Making a Character, Step by Step

  1. Frame and animal. Choose them, or roll a d6 for your frame (the table below) and a d6 for your animal: 1–2 is the first animal listed, 3–4 the second, 5–6 the third. No two characters in a group have the same animal. If two players want the same one, they each roll a die and the higher roll gets it (roll again on a tie); the other picks again (they can keep the same frame). Copy your frame’s animal ratings (Wits 0), its Strengths and Weaknesses, and your animal’s perk onto your sheet.
  2. Human ratings. Put 3, 2, 2, 1 and 1 into Sneak, Force, Move, Sense and Wits.
  3. Human perk. Choose one, or roll two d6.
  4. Your Mark. One feature that shows in both forms.
  5. The rest. A name, an age (11 to 15), and something you love about your town. Start every mission with 4 Wild and 0 Hurt.

The Five Approaches

Everything you try is done with one of five approaches. Each has a rating from 0 to 4: the number of dice you roll.

ApproachWhat it covers
SneakHiding, stalking, slipping past, staying unseen.
ForceFighting, breaking, shoving, scaring.
MoveRunning, climbing, jumping, flying, swimming.
SenseNoticing, tracking, searching, scouting.
WitsTalking, planning out loud, using tools and machines, reading. Human form only.

You have two sets of ratings. As a human, you use the five you chose in step 2. As an animal, you use your frame’s ratings instead, and your Wits is 0: animals can’t talk, read or use tools.

Places and Their Tags

Every place in a mission has one or more tags. Tags are how the world tells you which animals are at home there.

TagWhat it means
DarkUnlit or night-black: alleys, cellars, the woods at midnight.
HighRooftops, walls, trees, beams and ledges.
NarrowVents, pipes, crawlspaces, gaps under doors.
WaterRivers, moats, drains, pools.
OpenLawns, car parks, fields: wide spaces with no cover.
CrowdPeople around, who will notice an animal that doesn’t belong.
LockedDoors, cages, latches and machines.

In animal form, if any of your frame’s Strengths is among the place’s tags, you get +1 die. If any of its Weaknesses is, the roll is at +1 Difficulty. It’s one die and one step at most, however many tags match. If a place has both a Strength and a Weakness of yours, both apply. In human form, tags don’t change your roll.

Your frame is the kind of animal you become. Your animal is its real size: a moose is as big as a car and a mouse fits in a shoe. Roll a d6 or choose.

Animal-form Wits is always 0. Animal perks work only in animal form.

Big Cats

“You won’t hear me. You won’t see me. Then I’m on the shelf above your head.”

The best sneaks in the game, and good in a fight. Big Cats own the dark and the rooftops, but they hate water and they can’t blend into a crowd: people scream when a jaguar walks into the shop.

Jaguar · Night Rosettes

In Dark places, your Sneak rolls are at −1 Difficulty.

Snow Leopard · Ambush

Your first Strike in each Clash is at −1 Difficulty.

Cheetah · Burst

Once per scene, Push a Move roll without spending Wild.

Canines

“I smelled them before you saw them. They went that way. Come on!”

The best noses in the game, and solid at everything else. Canines run well in the open and pass for somebody’s dog in a crowd, but they’re no climbers.

Coyote · Pack

Once per scene, Help without spending Wild.

Shepherd Dog · Good Dog

In Crowds, your Sneak and Sense rolls are at −1 Difficulty. People see a dog, not you.

Red Fox · Wily

Once per scene, when your roll Fails, it becomes a Partial instead.

Birds

“From up here I can see the whole yard, the guard, the van, and the way out.”

Fast, far-sighted scouts who fly over walls and see everything from above. Birds are no good in a fight and hate tight spaces where they can’t open their wings.

Peregrine Falcon · Keen Eyes

In Open or High places, your Sense rolls are at −1 Difficulty.

Barn Owl · Silent Wings

In Dark places, +1 die on Sneak and Move rolls.

Raven · Clever Beak

Against Locked things you can roll Wits in animal form, with a rating of 2, to work latches, keys and switches. You still can’t talk.

Critters

“Nobody looks down. That’s the whole trick.”

Tiny, quick and everywhere. Critters slip through gaps nothing else fits through and scurry unnoticed under a crowd’s feet. Out in the open, everything wants to eat them, and they can’t fight at all.

House Mouse · Tiny

In Narrow places, your Sneak and Move rolls are at −1 Difficulty.

Brown Rat · Gnaw

Against Locked things, your Force rating is 3.

Grey Squirrel · Leaper

In High places, your Move rolls are at −1 Difficulty.

Heavies

“There was a door. There isn’t now.”

Huge and strong. Heavies break locks by breaking whatever the lock was attached to, and nothing stops them in the open. They don’t fit through anything narrow, and a bison in a crowd is not subtle.

Bison · Shaggy Coat

Once per scene, when you’d take Hurt, take 1 less.

Moose · Antler Sweep

Once per scene, when your Strike against a Beater or Hound Succeeds, it’s out at once.

Hippopotamus · Charge

Once per scene, when a Force roll Succeeds, it counts as a Critical.

Reptiles

“I can wait. I can wait all night.”

Patient all-rounders who are at their best in water and in the dark. People panic when a reptile turns up in a crowd.

Alligator · Lurker

In Water, your Sneak and Force rolls are at −1 Difficulty.

Python · Coil

When your Strike Succeeds, the next roll anyone makes against that threat this Clash is at −1 Difficulty.

Komodo Dragon · Taste the Air

+1 die on Sense rolls, and a Hound’s Tracker doesn’t affect you.

Human Perks

Your human perk is something you were good at before the Wild woke you, and it stays with you in both forms. Every perk works in any form unless it says otherwise. A perk’s extra die counts towards the Cap (Chapter 3). Choose one, or roll two d6: the first die picks the list (1–3 for A, 4–6 for B) and the second picks the row. Two players may have the same human perk.

2nd dieA (first die 1–3)B (first die 4–6)
1Football Player: +1 die on Force rolls.Team Captain: the team starts every mission with 1 Edge.
2Hide-and-Seek Champ: +1 die on Sneak rolls.Animal Whisperer: +1 die on rolls against Hounds, or to get past an obstacle they guard.
3Track Star: +1 die on Move rolls.Swim Team: +1 die on rolls in Water.
4Scout: +1 die on Sense rolls.Gymnast: +1 die on rolls in High places.
5Brainiac (human form): +1 die on Wits rolls.Night Owl: +1 die on rolls in Dark places.
6Gamer: start every mission with 1 extra Wild.Drama Kid: +1 die on rolls in Crowds.

Your Mark

Your Mark is the one thing about you that shows in both shapes: a freckled nose that becomes pale spots on a snout, a white streak of hair that becomes a white patch of fur, a scar through an eyebrow. Friends use it to tell which animal is you. So does the Hunt: when they learn your Mark, you’re Exposed (Chapter 7).

Wild and Hurt

You start every mission with 4 Wild tokens (5 with the Gamer perk) and 0 Hurt, with 3 Hurt boxes on your sheet. Wild is the fuel for everything special you do (Chapter 3). Spend it. Any you don’t spend is lost when the mission ends.

Example: Making Lucía

Lucía’s player rolls a 6 for her frame: Reptiles. Her animal die is a 2, the first animal listed, so she’s an alligator. She puts Sneak 2, Force 1, Move 3, Sense 1, Wits 2 for her human ratings: a fast swimmer and a fast talker. For her perk she rolls 4 and 3: list B, row 3, Swim Team. That gives +1 die on rolls in Water in either form, which suits an alligator very well. Her Mark is the freckles across her nose; as an alligator, they’re a spray of pale spots on her snout. She loves the river path behind the old mill.
