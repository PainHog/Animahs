Chapter 10

The Gilded Hunt

“Every wild thing has a price. Most of them simply haven’t been asked yet.” — overheard at the Lodge on Thornhill

The Gilded Hunt is older than the town, older than the roads, older than anyone’s great-grandmother’s stories. It began as a club of rich hunters who found out, long ago, that a Bonded animal caught alive and bound in gold gives up its gift to whoever wears the gold. They have been collecting ever since.

What the Hunt Wants

The Hunt wants the Wild itself. Every Trophy they make is a little piece of it, chained. For three hundred years they have been preparing for the Last Hunt: one night, under the right moon, when the Masters gather and bind the Old Wild for good. After that, every animal on Earth would obey whoever holds the leash, and the Hunt means to hold it. The people would follow soon after.

The Old Wild knows. That’s why it woke the Bonded.

Who They Are

Members of the Hunt are respectable. They sit on the museum board, own the mill, sponsor the zoo, give prizes at the school sports day. They are polite, generous and charming, and they never, ever talk about the Hunt in daylight. Nobody joins by accident. You’re invited, you’re tested, and you swear.

  • Masters lead the Hunt. Each wears several Trophies. They rarely leave the Lodge, and when they do, it’s because the Hunt is Up.
  • Huntsmen are full members. Each has earned one Trophy.
  • Beaters are hired hands: watchmen, drivers, “estate staff”. Most of them don’t know what the Hunt really is, and don’t want to.
  • Trappers are the Hunt’s specialists in catching things alive, with nets and cages.
  • Hounds are the Hunt’s own breed: tall, grey and silent, with gilt collars and noses that can smell the Wild.

Trophies

A Trophy is a Bonded animal’s gift, bound into a piece of gold: a ring, a brooch, a cane-head, a locket. Whoever wears it borrows that animal’s best sense or strength. Every Trophy was once somebody’s Bond. Break one, and the Bond goes free.

How the Hunt Finds You

The Hunt can smell the Wild. When the Bonded slip up, make noise, fight or shift in sight, the Hunt’s Scent grows (Chapter 5). The Hounds track it; the Trophies sharpen it; and when it’s strong enough, the Masters come. Between missions the Hunt is patient: it watches Exposed children, asks friendly questions at the school gate, and waits.

What the Hunt Does With the Taken

The Taken are caged, gently and coldly, like prize animals. The Hunt doesn’t hurt them; it needs them whole. At the next full moon, a Master binds the Taken child’s Bond into new gold. Until then, they can be rescued.
